Economic Policy

A stronger, more productive economy.

Liberty's economic programme is focused on creating the conditions for businesses to invest, workers to develop new skills, exporters to reach international markets and government to manage public finances sustainably.

Our approach draws on themes in the New Zealand National Party's economic platform: fiscal responsibility, investment, planning reform, trade, infrastructure and productivity.

The Budget figures below show the current financial framework supporting the programme. The policies describe intended priorities; they should not be read as proof that any particular economic outcome is guaranteed.

01 / Fiscal Responsibility

Sound public finances.

A credible fiscal framework helps government plan for the long term, manage borrowing and maintain confidence in public finances.

POLICY 01.1

Responsible Budget Management

Establish clear objectives for government debt, operating balances and expenditure growth. Publish regular updates on progress against these objectives.

Potential benefits

More transparent public finances and greater certainty when planning future spending.

POLICY 01.2

Value for Money

Regularly review government programmes to identify duplication, ineffective spending and opportunities to improve service delivery.

Potential benefits

Better value from public expenditure and more room to prioritise essential services.

02 / Business & Investment

Backing businesses to grow.

Investment in productive assets, technology and people can help businesses expand their capacity and improve productivity.

POLICY 02.1

Business Investment & Hiring Acceleration

Provide targeted support for eligible businesses investing in productive assets and expanding their workforce.

Budget allocation: $1.2 billion

Intended to support business expansion, new investment and additional employment opportunities.

POLICY 02.2

Small Business Compliance Relief

Reduce unnecessary administrative complexity and improve access to practical support for small and medium-sized businesses.

Budget allocation: $400 million

Intended to reduce compliance burdens and allow business owners to spend more time on productive activities.

POLICY 02.3

Investment Boost

Consider a tax incentive modelled on National's Investment Boost, allowing businesses to deduct an additional portion of qualifying new capital investment.

Potential benefits

Could reduce the effective upfront tax cost of investment and encourage businesses to modernise equipment and technology.

POLICY 02.4

Innovation and Technology

Support research commercialisation, technology adoption and investment in industries with potential to increase productivity.

Potential benefits

More innovation, more efficient production and opportunities for new industries.

03 / Planning & Regulation

Making it easier to build.

A clearer planning and consenting system can reduce uncertainty for businesses, infrastructure providers and developers.

POLICY 03.1

Resource Management Reform

Replace complex and overlapping planning requirements with a clearer system that provides consistent rules and more predictable consenting processes.

Potential benefits

Fewer unnecessary delays for housing, infrastructure and commercial development.

POLICY 03.2

Building Consent Reform

Review building consent processes and remove unnecessary duplication while retaining appropriate safety standards.

Potential benefits

More predictable project timelines and reduced administrative costs.

04 / Trade & Exports

Connecting New Zealand to the world.

Export growth can create opportunities for businesses to reach larger markets and diversify their customer base.

POLICY 04.1

Expanding Trade Relationships

Pursue trade agreements that improve market access and reduce unnecessary barriers for New Zealand exporters.

Potential benefits

More potential customers and a wider range of export markets.

POLICY 04.2

Supporting Exporters

Improve access to export information, international networks and support for businesses seeking to enter overseas markets.

Potential benefits

More opportunities for small and medium-sized firms to export.

POLICY 04.3

Agricultural Productivity

Encourage agricultural technology, research and productivity improvements across farming, forestry, fishing and food production.

Budget allocation: $1.5 billion

Supports investment in technology and productivity across primary industries.

POLICY 04.4

Regional Economic Development

Support regional infrastructure and investment projects that improve access to markets and strengthen local economies.

Budget allocation: $2.2 billion

Allocated to the Northern Prosperity Plan in the supplied Budget.

05 / Infrastructure & Energy

Building the foundations for growth.

Reliable transport, energy and public infrastructure can improve connections between workers, businesses and markets.

POLICY 05.1

Roads and Freight Corridors

Invest in strategic roads and freight routes that improve the movement of goods between ports, production centres and cities.

Budget allocation: $1.5 billion

Intended to improve freight connections and transport reliability.

POLICY 05.2

Public Transport Infrastructure

Upgrade urban transport networks and develop rapid transit connections in areas experiencing population growth.

Budget allocation: $2 billion

Allocated to bus rapid transit and urban transport upgrades.

POLICY 05.3

Energy Security

Encourage investment in reliable electricity generation, transmission and storage to support future energy demand.

Potential benefits

Greater electricity-system resilience and more certainty for businesses.

POLICY 05.4

Housing Supply Partnerships

Use public-private partnerships to support housing infrastructure and increase the supply of developable land and homes.

Budget allocation: $1.8 billion

Intended to support housing supply and related development.

06 / Skills & Productivity

Investing in people.

A skilled workforce is an important part of a productive economy. Training should help people develop skills that are useful to employers and adaptable to changing industries.

POLICY 06.1

Apprenticeship Wage Subsidy

Support eligible employers who take on apprentices and provide structured workplace training.

Budget allocation: $900 million

Intended to support apprenticeship opportunities and skills development.

POLICY 06.2

Fees-Free Vocational Training

Expand access to vocational education and technical qualifications in areas where skills are needed.

Budget allocation: $600 million

Intended to reduce financial barriers to vocational training.

Economic Outlook

How the programme is intended to work.

These are possible economic effects, not guaranteed results. Actual outcomes depend on implementation, economic conditions and the response of households and businesses.

More Investment

Business incentives and regulatory certainty may encourage investment in equipment, technology and productive assets.

Higher Productivity

Skills, technology and infrastructure can help workers and businesses produce more value from available resources.

Export Opportunities

Better trade access and exporter support may help domestic businesses reach more customers overseas.

Fiscal Policy Consideration

Tax policy and the economic platform.

National's published economic platform includes a commitment to no new taxes. The June 2026 Liberty Budget, however, includes several additional levies, including the Digital Platform Services Tax, Financial Transactions Stamp Duty, Private Natural Resource Extraction Levy and Foreign Workforce Employment Levy.

These are different policy choices. If Liberty intends to follow National's no-new-taxes approach, the revenue model and the proposed levies would need to be reviewed and reconciled before the platform is finalised.
