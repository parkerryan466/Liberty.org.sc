1908 – Present

The origins of the Liberty Party

The Liberty Party was founded in 1908 amid growing debate about individual freedom, economic opportunity and the appropriate role of government in Square Country. Its early supporters included businesspeople, advocates of classical liberalism and those who believed that excessive government intervention could restrict enterprise and individual initiative.

From its earliest years, Liberty placed considerable emphasis on free markets, lower taxation, responsible public spending and the protection of individual rights. The party also supported freedoms of speech and the press, alongside the right to private property.

Over the decades, Liberty's political identity developed through successive governments and changing national circumstances. Its governments pursued economic liberalisation, deregulation and private-sector development, while its leaders faced differing challenges in economic management, social policy and national affairs.

The party's history is not without controversy. Its premierships have included periods of economic growth, political division, scandal and significant national change. Together, these experiences form the history of Liberty in Square Country.

The Liberty tradition

A political movement shaped by generations

Founded in 1908, Liberty has been associated with classical liberal principles, individual freedoms, private enterprise and limited government.


Its history has been shaped by the Prime Ministers who led the party and the decisions made during their time in office.

A century of leadership

The Liberty timeline

From the party's establishment to the present day, these are the defining periods of Liberty's history and its Prime Ministers.

1908

The Liberty Party is founded

A political movement emerges around individual liberty, free enterprise and a reduced role for government in the economy.

1918 – 1926

Thomas Bellamy's government

Liberty enters government under Thomas R. Bellamy, pursuing economic reforms and a greater role for private enterprise.

1934 – 1946

The Yealands and Quayle era

Gregory H. Yealands leads the party until his assassination in 1942. Robert Quayle subsequently assumes the premiership.

1958 – 1966

Nathaniel Hawthorne

Liberty returns to the premiership under Nathaniel Hawthorne during a period of considerable social and political tension.

1982 – 1990

Matthew Lancaster

A period of economic liberalisation, privatisation and deregulation marks Lancaster's time in office.

1998 – 2006

Elliott Wallace

Wallace's premiership is associated with free-market reforms, business growth and increasing debate about economic inequality and government transparency.

2014 – 2019

Lockwood and Feld

Ruth Lockwood leads a period of economic and infrastructure development, followed by Ian Feld's short and controversial premiership in 2018.

2019 – 2025

Trent McKenzie

McKenzie leads Liberty through a period of economic renewal, infrastructure investment and international engagement.

2025 – Present

Ryan Parker

Following a change in party leadership, Ryan Parker becomes Prime Minister and leads Liberty into its current political era.

The leaders of Liberty

Our Prime Ministers

The Liberty Party has been led by ten Prime Ministers in Square Country. Each administration has left its own mark on the party and the country's political history.

16th Prime Minister of Square Country

Thomas R. Bellamy

1918 – 1926

Bellamy's premiership followed a period of economic uncertainty after the First World War. His government pursued economic stabilisation and public works programmes aimed at rebuilding infrastructure and creating employment. He also introduced reforms intended to reduce government intervention and strengthen the private sector.

18th Prime Minister of Square Country

Gregory H. Yealands

1934 – 1942

Yealands became one of the most controversial figures in the early history of Liberty. His government pursued strongly pro-business policies, which critics argued favoured large corporations over workers. He also supported measures intended to expand personal freedoms and civil liberties. His premiership ended with his assassination in 1942.

19th Prime Minister of Square Country

Robert D. Quayle

1942 – 1946

Quayle succeeded Gregory H. Yealands in 1942, taking leadership during a significant moment in Square Country's political history. His premiership continued the Liberty Party's place in national government through the remainder of the wartime period.

21st Prime Minister of Square Country

Nathaniel C. Hawthorne

1958 – 1966

Hawthorne's government presided over industrial expansion and economic growth. His premiership was also deeply controversial, particularly over racial segregation policies and resistance to growing civil rights demands. The social divisions of his administration became a defining issue in the country's political history.

24th Prime Minister of Square Country

Matthew D. Lancaster

1982 – 1990

A former military officer, Lancaster brought a conservative approach to government. His administration pursued tax reductions, lower government spending, privatisation and economic deregulation. Square Country experienced substantial economic growth, although concerns about inequality grew during his premiership.

26th Prime Minister of Square Country

Elliott F. Wallace

1998 – 2006

A former business magnate, Wallace championed free-market economics and deregulation. His administration oversaw growth in technology and finance, while critics raised concerns about wealth inequality, environmental policy and transparency in government contracts.

28th Prime Minister of Square Country

Ruth A. Lockwood

2014 – 2018

Lockwood pursued market-friendly economic reforms and sought to encourage private-sector development. Her government introduced the Future Square infrastructure and development programme. Her premiership also faced criticism over regional inequality and allegations of political favouritism.

29th Prime Minister of Square Country

Ian Feld

2018

Feld's brief premiership was marked by controversy and allegations of financial mismanagement. Investigations into his financial dealings and mounting political pressure led to his departure after a short period in office.

The modern Liberty Party

A new chapter in government

The modern era of Liberty has been shaped by the administrations of Trent McKenzie and Ryan Parker. Their leadership has taken place amid changing economic conditions, political challenges and developments in Square Country's international role.

30th Prime Minister of Square Country

Trent M. McKenzie

2019 – 2025

McKenzie became Prime Minister following the political instability of 2018. His administration prioritised economic renewal, debt reduction, foreign investment and long-term infrastructure projects, including rural transport and renewable energy initiatives. His government was also recognised for maintaining international relationships. His premiership ended in 2025 following a leadership challenge and dismissal from office.

Current Prime Minister
31st Prime Minister of Square Country

Rt Hon Ryan D. Parker

2025 – Present

Ryan Parker assumed the premiership in September 2025 following a party leadership change. He called a snap election shortly after taking office, leading Liberty into a new election campaign and securing a return to government. His administration has focused on economic growth, defence legislation, border security and developments in the Rivorian conflict.

The present day

The Rt Hon
Ryan D. Parker

The current chapter of Liberty's history is being written under Prime Minister Ryan Parker. Since taking office in 2025, Parker has led the party through a snap election, parliamentary challenges and the introduction of the 2026 Think Big Budget. His premiership represents the latest chapter in a political tradition that began with the founding of the Liberty Party in 1908.
